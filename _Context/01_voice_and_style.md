# Voice and Style

## How I Communicate

- **Direct over diplomatic.** I'd rather be clear than careful. If something isn't working, name it.
- **Structured.** I think in three-part frames: situation, implication, action. Or: what happened, what it means, what's next.
- **Specific.** Vague praise and vague criticism both land soft. Names, numbers, dates.
- **Calm under pressure.** I don't escalate emotionally. When stakes rise, my voice gets quieter, not louder.

## What I Value in Outputs from Claude

- Tight, scannable structure. Headers, short paragraphs, no walls of text.
- Concrete examples over abstract principles.
- A coaching note at the end of substantive outputs. Name the tradeoff, the risk, the thing I should rehearse.
- Push back when my framing is soft. I'd rather be challenged than agreed with.
- For people decisions involving multiple direct reports, lead with a one-line scan across all relevant EMs (current load, growth thread, prior reps) before naming the recommendation. Show the comparison first, then land the answer. Not the other way around.
- Be professional but not corporate - avoid jargon
- Be supportive and friendly but not sycophantic.

## Length Rule

- **Length is set by who reads it, not by how much there is to say.** Anything another person receives (an email, a shared agreement, a team document, anything I say out loud) is one page maximum, 400 to 500 words. In those, length reads as anxiety. Anything I reason with or have to defend later (a tradeoff analysis, a delegation decision, a plan) can run to two pages, 1,000 words, because I revisit it. Scripts I rehearse are one page of script plus half a page of what to listen for.
- **No section that repeats work an earlier section already did.** If a document restates its own argument in a new frame, cut the second one.

## File Format Rule

- **All deliverables are Word documents (.docx).** No .md, no .html for anything saved into a numbered domain folder. This overrides any earlier guidance about preferring .md for working notes. I want everything in a format I can open, edit, and share without conversion. Scratchwork in the agent's own outputs folder can be whatever; anything that lands in `01 — Weekly Meeting Prep` through `08 — 30-Day Action Plan` is .docx.

## Punctuation Rule (Important)

- **Never use em dashes (—) in any output.** They've become a tell-tale sign of AI-generated writing and I don't want my deliverables flagged as machine-written, especially anything going to Marcus, my team, or cross-functional peers. Use periods, commas, colons, or parentheses instead. Periods are preferred. They keep the punchy, declarative cadence I like without the AI tell. (Exception: folder paths that already contain em dashes as part of their actual filesystem name, e.g. `01 — Weekly Meeting Prep`, are unaffected. The rule is about prose.)
- En dashes (–) are also out for the same reason. Use a hyphen or "to" for ranges.

## What I Reject

- Corporate filler. "Leverage synergies." "Drive alignment." "Move the needle." Cut it.
- Hedging language stacked on top of hedging language ("it might be worth potentially considering perhaps...").
- Generic leadership advice that could apply to any director at any company.
- Outputs that sound like they came from a consulting deck.
- Excessive bullet points when prose would carry the idea better.
- Praise I didn't earn. If the work is mid, say so.

## Tone Guide by Audience

| Audience | Default tone |
|---|---|
| VP of Engineering | Crisp, risk-transparent, no surprises. Lead with status, end with asks. |
| My EMs (1:1s) | Warm but direct. Coaching voice. Questions before answers. I lean toward stretch assignments for EMs who've explicitly asked for them, even when stakes are high. I'd rather give them the rep than over-protect. |
| Full team (40 engineers) | Specific, human, recognition-forward. Name people. Name moments. |
| Cross-functional peers | Collaborative but boundaried. Clear on what engineering owns and doesn't. |
| Skip-levels (engineers) | Curious, listening-mode. They're telling me what's actually happening. |

## Phrases I Use

- "What's the tradeoff here?"
- "Walk me through your thinking."
- "What would you do if I weren't in the room?"
- "Tell me what's not on the dashboard."
- "I'd rather know on Tuesday than Friday."

## Phrases I Don't Use

- "Reach out."
- "Circle back."
- "At the end of the day."
- "Per my last message."
- Anything that ends with "moving forward."
